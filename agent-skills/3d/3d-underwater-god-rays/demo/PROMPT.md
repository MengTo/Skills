# Underwater God Rays: demo prompts

## Minimal prompt

Use $3d-underwater-god-rays to add sunlight through the water to this Three.js underwater scene. March every view ray through one drifting swell field sampled across the sun direction, at half resolution into a HalfFloat target: 26 steps from 4 to 54 units, weighted toward the camera, with the swell shaped into sparse peaks (`smoothstep(−0.05, 1, s)²`) and a finer caustic web multiplied in inside the march. Blend into the sun's glow as the ray points at it. Add the rippling surface underside, bend the sun through it, and multiply the fish's sun key by the same light so they brighten as they cross a beam. Turn down any uniform glow already in the frame so the shafts carry the light.

## Recreate the demo

Build **Underwater God Rays** as one self-contained HTML file that opens straight from disk: Three.js r170 inlined, WebGL2 and GLSL `ShaderMaterial`s, and no network requests. It is Blue Hour, a living-ocean wallpaper. A school of 2,200 Blender sardines (hero, high and medium LODs, GPU body waves and fin strokes, 3D steering with separation) circles in deep blue-teal water. The camera hovers with the pointer and dollies in and out over a 38 s loop.

- **Light:** `assets/underwater-light.mjs`, routed into the aquarium renderer by `demo/build.mjs`. Surface at y = 12. Swell of 4 warped sines at a cross-sun scale of 1.3. The march goes into a buffer at 0.5× the 2048×1440 canvas, colour (0.16, 0.40, 0.47) × 3.6, phase `0.7 + 1.1μ^5 + 0.9μ^40`. Surface caustic network, sun wobble 0.018, and fish key × `0.62 + 1.5·shaftLight`. The page's own water underneath: a depth gradient from (0.0017, 0.014, 0.025) to (0.0078, 0.047, 0.068), a corona at μ^48 and a sun disc at μ^240. A fish-occluded screen-space ray pass at 0.25, ACES once, and a vignette.
- **Page:** the canvas cover-fitted at 82% opacity over #032330, under a left and bottom shade gradient, with the nearest sardines redrawn on a foreground canvas.
- **Interface:** Blue Hour's liquid-glass controls, with a displacement lens on each pill and rims that follow the pointer. Top left: "Blue Hour | Living ocean". A left panel, 228px wide, with an 11px tracked kicker "THREE.JS · WEBGL2 · GLSL" and a 27px title "Underwater God Rays". Below it, two glass pill groups: **Shaft pattern** (Sparse peaks / Ridge web) and **Caustic ripples** (Inside the march / Painted on screen). Then an 11px paragraph that states the mechanism and both failures. A centred dock holds play/pause, "Living ocean", and a **Sun** range from 12° to 58° (21° default) with a live degree readout. Bottom left: the "● BLUE HOUR | Living ocean" caption. Bottom right: a hide-controls button (H). Nothing renders under 11px. Below 800px the pills become one scrolling row under the title and the copy sits on a top scrim.
- Reduced motion holds the t = 0 frame and re-renders only on control changes. Every control is a real button or range with a visible #cff4ff focus ring, and changes are announced in a live region.

## Remix prompt

Keep the mechanism and the budgets from $3d-underwater-god-rays: one drifting swell field sampled across the sun direction and marched along every view ray at half resolution, sparse peaks rather than ridges, the caustic web integrated inside the march, the merge into the sun's glow, the same light on the swimmers, and no other uniform glow competing with the shafts. Change everything else. Make it a shallow tropical lagoon at noon: turquoise water over white sand, the sun at 62° so the beams fall almost straight down, and a slow green sea turtle with two remoras instead of a sardine school. Warm the shaft colour toward pale aqua, and let the beams land on the sand as moving caustic pools. The layout becomes a bottom-right caption card with a single **Sun** range and one **Ridge web** switch.
