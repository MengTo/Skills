# The the design tool prompts, word for word

Every prompt typed into the design tool for the three demos in the video, in the order they were used. Each prompt made four designs; one was picked, and the next prompt was added below it as a new section.

## Demo 1 · Tarnfield (a landing page, six sections)

### Hero

A landing page for Tarnfield, an alpine field guide app for one glacial basin. Hero: a photoreal granite mountain range at first light, reflected in a still glacial lake, cool blue-grey shadows with warm peach sunrise on the peaks. An enormous bold white grotesk headline "Morning." spans the full width and sits behind the summit, so the peaks overlap the letters. A real iPhone stands upright in the lake at the centre, showing the app: an EAST BASIN station list with altitudes and a Start the walk button. Left column under the headline: "Your alpine field guide." with two lines of small copy and an Explore the basin link. Right column: "A day on the mountain." with two lines of copy and a View the plates link. Minimal top bar: a small Tarnfield wordmark with a sun glyph on the left, nav The basin, Transect, Plates, Field notes, and an outlined Download pill. A thin progress rail with station ticks along the bottom edge. Calm, cinematic and editorial, lots of air, no cards, no gradients.

### Section 2 · The basin map

The basin map. The page continues into a dark top-down relief map of the same basin: graphite rock, pale snow fields and a glacial lake, with thin contour lines and six station markers joined by a dotted trail. The same iPhone floats in the centre, now showing the app's map screen with the route drawn on it and a Start the walk button. Left of the phone: a large "01" with "At the lake" and one short line of copy. Right of the phone: "02" with "East basin" and one short line. Small monospace altitude labels beside each station. Same type and quiet spacing as the hero.

### Section 3 · The transect

The transect. A near-black section with fine engraved contour lines. On the right, a floating 3D terrain block of the basin cut out like a museum model: granite ridges, snow line, the lake, the layered rock visible on its cut sides, lit by a low warm sun. On the left: a small monospace label "03 / THE TRANSECT", a large headline "Snow line." in the same grotesk, and two lines of copy about walking from the lake to the col. Below it, a thin elevation profile graph from 2,320 m to 3,540 m with station dots, and an "Explore in 3D" link. Same quiet editorial spacing.

### Section 4 · Study plates

Study plates. The page turns to warm off-white paper like a printed field journal. A small monospace label "04 / STUDY PLATES", a large dark headline "Study plates." and one line of copy on the right. Below, three tall photographic plates side by side with thin black hairline frames: a close-up of lichen on a granite edge, a snowy saddle between two peaks, and the lake's broken reflection in wind. Under each plate a small caption with a filled dot: Granite edge, East saddle, Broken reflection, with the altitude in monospace. Generous margins, like a museum catalogue.

### Section 5 · Field notes

Field notes. The light turns to dusk: a full-bleed photograph of the same granite peaks glowing rose and violet after sunset, deep purple shadows in the valley, the first stars. Over the left third, a monospace label "05 / FIELD NOTES", a large white headline "Field notes." and three short dated journal entries in small white type, each with a time and an altitude, separated by hairlines. On the right, a slim vertical ruler of altitude ticks with a small marker at 2,960 m. Calm, quiet and cinematic, same type as the hero.

### Section 6 · Night

Night field sheet, the last section and footer. Deep navy night over the same basin: the ridgeline as a dark silhouette, a bright full moon, a few stars, the lake holding the moonlight. On the left, a monospace label "06 / NIGHT", a large white headline "Field sheet 017." and two lines of copy about carrying the whole basin offline in your pocket, with a solid white "Download Tarnfield" pill and an outlined "View the plates" pill. Below, a slim footer on the same navy: the Tarnfield wordmark with its sun glyph, the nav The basin, Transect, Plates, Field notes, and a tiny line "Made for one basin. Six stations. 2,320 to 3,540 m."

## Demo 2 · Folvra (a landing page, five sections, with image references)

### Hero

A landing page for Folvra, a treasury app that keeps one set of books per client commission for independent creative studios. Use the attached painting as the hero art and the attached logo sheet for every logo. Neoclassical layout on warm cream laid paper with faint engraved banknote watermarks in the margins. Left half: a tiny monospace label "// THE STUDIO LEDGER", an enormous tight black grotesk headline "Commission", a serif subhead "Follow a studio commission from the first advance to the final payment.", a hairline rule, a monospace line "ARCHITECTURAL STUDY · $24,800 FEE", a dark espresso pill button "RECORD $9,600 ADVANCE" and a text link "Open the ledger" with a down arrow. Right half: the attached painting cut out on the paper with no frame. Top bar: the folvra wordmark from the logo sheet, centred nav Ledger, Workflow, Handoffs, Studio notes, a dark DEMO pill and an outlined INQUIRE pill. Bottom: a tiny monospace caption "STUDIOS IN THE DEMO" over the six studio logos from the sheet: Ternwick, Veysett, Orriven, Sablequay, Elmora, Brindlefold. Espresso, terracotta and slate blue on cream, no gradients, no glass.

### Section 2

The ledger. A wide unframed oil painting of a calm Mediterranean harbour at golden hour fills the section edge to edge: a stone boathouse on a wooden jetty, a moored boat with a red sail, still water, soft clouds. Over its left side floats a dark smoked-glass app panel titled "Studio ledger" with the folvra wordmark, a STUDIO DEMO tag and Q3 Q2 Q1 tabs; tabs Balance, Receipts, Invoice; AVAILABLE FUNDS $84,760.52; three small stats Client receipts, Production costs, Opening funds; three envelopes OPS Operating, JOB Committed, BUF Reserve with amounts; a JUL 1 to SEP 30 footer. Above the painting on the cream paper: a monospace label "// PROJECT ACCOUNTS", a large grotesk headline "Ledger" and one line of serif copy on the right.

### Section 3

Workflow. Back on the cream laid paper. Left: a monospace label "// WORKING METHODS", an enormous tight grotesk headline "Workflow", two lines of serif copy about keeping the client's advance, supplier bills and crew payments together from the first estimate through delivery, and a dark espresso pill button with a coin icon "SEE THE WORKFLOW". Right half: a Renaissance oil painting cut out on the paper with no frame: a young woman in a red headwrap and a slate-blue gown with a gold chain, reclining on the floor among heavy drapery, typing on an open modern laptop whose screen shows a dark finance dashboard. Same palette and type as the hero.

### Section 4

Handoffs. On the cream laid paper, a wide unframed painterly study in the same Neoclassical style: from the left, a polished steel robot hand with visible joints reaches in, and from the right, a human hand in a terracotta sleeve; between them a small leather coin pouch has just opened and gold coins spill into the human palm. Above the image: a monospace label "// HANDOFFS", an enormous tight grotesk headline "Handoffs" and one line of serif copy about paying the crew the moment a milestone is approved. Under the image, three columns with monospace labels and amounts: Advance received $9,600, Crew paid $6,200, Balance due $15,200. Same palette and type as the hero.

### Section 5

The closing invitation and footer. On the cream laid paper, a wide Neoclassical arch drawn like an engraving frames a painted scene: the standing man in terracotta and the seated woman in slate blue from the hero, now shaking hands under the arch, the contract signed on the stone between them. Centred above: a monospace label "// OPEN YOUR STUDIO LEDGER", a big grotesk headline "Start the next commission", one serif line, and two pills: a dark espresso "BOOK A DEMO" and an outlined "INQUIRE". Below, a quiet footer: the folvra wordmark, columns Ledger, Workflow, Handoffs, Studio notes, a small engraved coin seal and "Treasury for independent studios".

## Demo 3 · Brasswater Foundry (a game: the battle screen, then two pages from it)

### Battle screen

The battle screen of an original 16-bit pixel-art browser RPG called "Brasswater Foundry". Side-view, turn-based battle inside a flooded clockwork foundry: copper pipes, amber furnace light, rising steam, a wet iron grate floor with reflections and dripping water. On the left, three original party members in detailed pixel art, facing right: Wren, a lancer in a teal long coat with a hooked spear; Oda, a lantern mage holding a glass lantern staff that glows blue; Tamsin, a clockwork engineer with a wrench-hammer and a small hovering drone. On the right, the boss: the Foundry Warden, a hulking brass-and-iron beetle machine with a glowing furnace belly and two pincer arms, with a small rivet drone beside it. UI: a gold-trimmed title plate top-left reading BRASSWATER FOUNDRY; a boss plate top-right with its name and a segmented HP bar; under the scene a row of four large command buttons with pixel icons, Attack, Arts, Item and Guard, with Arts highlighted; below that three party cards with portrait, HP, MP and a BURST gauge; a status line "Wren is ready. Choose a command." and TURN 03 at the bottom right. Crisp pixel art, chunky readable pixel font, dark UI panels with brass trim.

### Page 2

The title screen of the same game, in the same pixel-art style and palette. The flooded foundry seen from outside at dusk: a huge brass furnace hall half under water, chimneys with amber light, rain, a narrow iron walkway in front. Wren, Oda and Tamsin stand small on the walkway with their backs to us, looking up at the hall. A large ornate pixel logo "BRASSWATER FOUNDRY" in brass lettering with rivets and a teal enamel inlay, a small subtitle "A turn-based tale of steam and salt". Below it a vertical menu in the same brass-trimmed panels: New Game, Continue, Party, Settings, with New Game highlighted, and a blinking "Press Enter" hint. Version number bottom left, a small studio mark bottom right.

### Page 3

A character and enemy reference sheet for the same game, for modelling them in 3D. Warm grey paper background with fine grid lines, labelled like a production model sheet. Top row: Wren the lancer shown front, side and back in a neutral A-pose, full body, teal long coat, leather straps, hooked spear beside him. Middle row: Oda the lantern mage and Tamsin the clockwork engineer, each front, side and back in an A-pose, with their lantern staff, wrench-hammer and drone drawn separately. Bottom row: the Foundry Warden boss machine shown front, side, back and three-quarter, with callouts for the furnace belly, pincer arms, brass plates and rivets, and the small rivet drone. Painterly, clean silhouettes, consistent proportions, height lines in metres, small colour swatches for each character. No background scene.

## Demo 3 · Brasswater Foundry, the long scroll (three sections under one battle design)

### Section 1

The party. The page continues below the battle screen into a dark panel with brass trim over the same flooded foundry, the steam thinning and amber light from above. A small pixel label "01 / THE PARTY" and a large chunky pixel headline "Three went in." Below, three tall character cards side by side in the same pixel-art style, each with a full-body sprite on a warm amber backdrop: Wren the lancer in a teal long coat with a hooked spear, Oda the lantern mage with a glowing blue lantern staff, Tamsin the clockwork engineer with a wrench-hammer and a small hovering drone. Under each card: the name, the role, one short line, and three small segmented bars for HP, MP and BURST. Same pixel font, dark panels and brass trim as the battle UI.

### Section 2

The Foundry Warden. A deeper section, almost black, lit only by the boss's furnace glow from below. On the right, the Foundry Warden large in detailed pixel art: the hulking brass-and-iron beetle machine with its glowing furnace belly and two pincer arms, steam rising off it, the small rivet drone circling. On the left, a small pixel label "02 / THE BOSS", a large pixel headline "The Foundry Warden.", two short lines about the machine that keeps the flooded foundry running, and a list of its three attacks, each with a pixel icon and one short line: Pincer Sweep, Boiler Burst, Rivet Swarm. A long segmented boss HP bar runs across the bottom of the section. Same pixel font and brass-trimmed panels.

### Section 3

How a turn plays, the last section and footer. The foundry at dawn after the battle: the water calm, pale amber light through high arched windows, the three heroes small on the iron walkway. A small pixel label "03 / HOW A TURN PLAYS" and four numbered steps in a row in brass-trimmed panels, each with a pixel icon and one short line: Choose a command, Spend MP on Arts, Fill your BURST, Break the Warden. Below, a large brass "Play in your browser" button with rivets and a teal enamel inlay, and a smaller outlined "Watch a turn" button. Then a slim footer: the BRASSWATER FOUNDRY pixel logo, links Party, The Boss, How to play, Devlog, and a tiny line "An original turn-based tale of steam and salt. Plays in any browser."
